Flight profile · scroll to fly

From pad to hoverslam

Four approximate public milestones from a Falcon 9 flight. The line fills as you move through the sequence; the numbers are a study, not live telemetry.

T-00:38 · Ignition

  1. 01 / Pad 39AT-00:38

    Ignition

    Nine Merlin engines reach full thrust while the hold-down clamps keep the stack on the pad. The release is a single mechanical decision; the rest of the flight is a balance of propellant and timing.

    Propellant
    RP-1 / LOX
    Engines
    9 × Merlin 1D
  2. 02 / Max QT+02:32

    Stage separation

    The first stage shuts down and separates. Cold-gas thrusters flip the booster for its return burn while the second-stage Merlin pushes Dragon toward orbit.

    Booster
    Return burn
    Payload
    Crew Dragon
  3. 03 / Re-entryT+06:30

    Entry burn

    Three engines light against the direction of travel to slow the first stage before the atmosphere does the rest. Grid fins take over the steering as the booster falls back toward the coast.

    Engines
    3 × Merlin
    Control
    Grid fins
  4. 04 / Landing zoneT+08:24

    Landing burn

    A single engine lights for the hoverslam: too early wastes propellant, too late ends the booster. The legs deploy and the stage meets the ground at zero velocity, not zero altitude.

    Target

Engineering notes

SpaceX, with a Star Wars twist

Every mission is a real aerospace trade-off wearing a Rebellion paint job.

Methalox, not magic

Falcon 9 flies on RP-1 and liquid oxygen; Starship moves to subcooled methane and oxygen. Mission 01 borrows that choice, then adds a gas from Bespin.

The hoverslam

A Falcon booster cannot hover. It lights one engine and meets the deck at zero velocity. Mission 02 hands that exact profile to a landing droid over Kamino.

Shields are mass

Dragon uses PICA-X, Starship uses tiles. Mission 03 asks the same question with Beskar: cover everything, cover the leading edges, or carry nothing.

Separate desktop build

The simulator is its own room

No game is embedded in this page. The playable Interstellar Driver's Ed is a single-file Python desktop app with three flight profiles, a local pilot record and no network calls. This archive stays readable on its own; the simulator opens when you choose to download it.
